Space Orbit Visualization Reference

Welcome to the Space Orbit Simulation reference page. This interactive display functions as an educational model showing the physical and aesthetic transitions associated with orbital mechanics and space operations.

Interactive Stages:

  • Launch: Demonstrates high gravitational loads (rising G-forces) and combustion thrust represented by high-velocity downward thermal particles. The ground coordinate graphs show vertical acceleration.
  • Orbit: Demonstrates circular orbital motion and microgravity (zero G). The particles align into an orbital accretion disk, illustrating how objects maintain orbital velocity to stay aloft.
  • Warp: Demonstrates hypothetical hyperdrive or warp speed. A cylinder particle warp-tunnel creates the illusion of speed trails due to linear camera perspective deformation and extreme FOV shift.

Visual and Physics Logic:

  • GSAP Interpolation: When switching phases, GSAP mathematically morphs all 12,000 particles by interpolating their positions, velocities, and colors between coordinate engines, preventing jumpy frame shifts.
  • Procedural Particle Generation: To ensure performance, particles are loaded into a single GPU BufferGeometry. White stars drift while colored engines run customized mathematical loops (plume vectors, circular orbit rotation, linear tunnel reset).
  • Dynamic Synthesizer Audio: Uses the Web Audio API to procedurally generate sound waves. Launch triggers physical bass oscillators and noise hums; Orbit shifts to a calm chord loop; Warp generates dynamic sweeping frequencies.

Designed for class project presentations. Press [SPACE] to pause/play and [1, 2, 3] to hotkey phase transitions.
